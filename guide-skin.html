THE INGREDIENTS — EXPLAINED
🫧
Ingredient 01

Salicylic Acid (BHA)

Clogged pores Blackheads Whiteheads
What It Does

Salicylic acid is oil-soluble, so it gets inside the pore and loosens the mix of oil and dead skin cells that clogs it. It's also mildly anti-inflammatory. Those tiny dots on your nose are often sebaceous filaments, not blackheads. They're a normal part of your pores and they come back. Salicylic acid keeps them less visible but won't make them disappear.

Look For

0.5–2% in a face wash or a leave-on serum or toner

How to Use
1Face wash: once a day is enough. Leave the lather on for 30–60 seconds before rinsing.
2Leave-on: start 2–3 nights a week and increase only if your skin stays comfortable
3Always follow with moisturiser, and wear sunscreen the next morning
⚠️ Don't use it on the same night as a retinoid or other exfoliating acids. Avoid it if you're allergic to aspirin. If you're pregnant, check with your dermatologist first.
🎯
Ingredient 02

Azelaic Acid

Dark spots Acne marks Post-acne pigmentation
What It Does

It slows down excess pigment production, calms inflammation and helps control acne, so it treats the mark and the breakout that caused it. It's gentle enough for most skin types, including sensitive and acne-prone skin.

Look For

10% over the counter. 15–20% strengths are prescription only.

How to Use
1Once a day, morning or night, on clean, dry skin
2Mild tingling or itching in the first 1–2 weeks is common and usually settles
3Once your skin is used to it, you can go up to twice a day
4Wear sunscreen every morning, or the spots will keep coming back
⏳ Give it 8–12 weeks. If a dark patch keeps returning on your cheeks or forehead, it could be melasma, which needs a dermatologist's plan.
🧱
Ingredient 03

Ceramides

Damaged skin barrier Dry, flaky skin
What It Does

Ceramides are the "cement" between your skin cells and make up about half of the fats in your skin's outer layer. When they run low, water escapes and irritants get in. That's when products start to sting, skin feels tight after washing, and flaking and redness show up.

Look For

A moisturiser with ceramides plus cholesterol and fatty acids. These three work best together.

How to Use
1Twice a day on slightly damp skin, right after cleansing
2While your barrier repairs, pause scrubs, exfoliating acids and strong actives for 2–4 weeks
3Keep it minimal: gentle cleanser → ceramide moisturiser → sunscreen
💡 If everything you apply suddenly stings, your barrier is asking for a break, not another active.
🌙
Ingredient 04

Retinoids

Fine lines Texture Early ageing
What It Does

Retinoids are vitamin A derivatives. They speed up cell turnover and stimulate collagen, which makes them the most studied topical ingredient for fine lines. Retinol and retinal are available over the counter. Adapalene and tretinoin are prescription strength and should be used under a dermatologist.

Look For

Start low with something like 0.1–0.3% retinol. Strength matters less than consistency.

How to Use
1Night only, using a pea-sized amount for the whole face
2Start with 2 nights a week and build up slowly over several weeks
3If your skin is dry or sensitive, apply moisturiser first and the retinoid on top
4Wear sunscreen every morning, because retinoids make skin more sun-sensitive
⚠️ Don't use if you're pregnant, trying to conceive or breastfeeding. Dryness and peeling in the first 4–6 weeks are common. Visible results take 3–6 months.
🌿
Ingredient 05

Niacinamide

Redness-prone skin Uneven skin tone Oiliness
What It Does

Niacinamide is vitamin B3. It helps your skin make more of its own ceramides, and a stronger barrier means less reactivity and redness. It also reduces how much pigment reaches the surface, which evens out tone, and it helps regulate oil.

Look For

2–5% is well studied and well tolerated. Higher isn't automatically better, and 10% or more can irritate sensitive skin.

How to Use
1Morning, night or both
2Pairs well with most ingredients, including vitamin C, retinoids and sunscreen
3Give it 8–12 weeks to show results for tone
💡 For very reactive skin, Centella asiatica (cica) is another good calming option. If you have constant redness, flushing or burning, it could be rosacea, so get it diagnosed instead of layering products.
🍊
Ingredient 06

Vitamin C

Dull-looking skin Antioxidant protection
What It Does

Vitamin C is an antioxidant that neutralises free radicals from sun and pollution. It brightens skin and supports collagen. It works best in the morning under sunscreen, where it boosts your protection but doesn't replace it.

Look For

L-ascorbic acid at 10–20% is the most studied but can sting. For sensitive skin, try a gentler form such as ethyl ascorbic acid or sodium ascorbyl phosphate.

How to Use
1In the morning, apply 3–4 drops to clean, dry skin
2Follow with moisturiser and sunscreen
3Store it away from heat and light. If it turns dark orange or brown, it has oxidised, so replace it.
💡 Mild tingling is common, but burning isn't. If it burns, switch to a gentler form.
💧
Ingredient 07

Hyaluronic Acid

Dehydrated skin All skin types
What It Does

Hyaluronic acid is a humectant, meaning it pulls water into the skin's surface and holds it there. Dehydrated skin isn't the same as dry skin: dehydrated skin lacks water and can happen even if you're oily, while dry skin lacks oil.

Look For

A serum or moisturiser with hyaluronic acid or sodium hyaluronate. It's suitable for every skin type, including oily skin.

How to Use
1Apply to slightly damp skin, morning and night
2Seal it in with a moisturiser straight after
💡 On its own, especially in a dry, air-conditioned room, it can leave your skin feeling tighter. That's why the moisturiser on top matters.
☀️
The Non-Negotiable

Broad-spectrum Sunscreen

Sun damage Tanning Protecting every result above
What It Does

Sunscreen blocks the UVA and UVB rays behind tanning, pigmentation, sunburn and early ageing. Without it, no pigmentation ingredient can keep up and the spots return. Retinoids and acids also make your skin more sun-sensitive.

Look For

SPF 30 or higher (SPF 50 for Indian summers or long hours outdoors), labelled broad-spectrum, with PA+++ or above

How to Use
1Every morning, including indoors near windows and on cloudy days
2Use the two-finger rule: two finger-lengths of sunscreen for your face and neck
3Reapply every 2–3 hours outdoors, and after sweating or swimming
"An ingredient name on the front of a bottle tells you very little. What matters is how much of it is inside, how it's made, and whether it matches your skin."

Before you buy anything for your concern, check these four things.

BEFORE YOU BUY — 4 THINGS TO CHECK
1

Concentration

0.5% and 2% salicylic acid are very different products. Too little may do nothing, and too much may irritate. If a label doesn't mention the percentage, be cautious.

2

Formulation

A face wash stays on your skin for seconds, while a serum stays on all day. The base, the pH and the packaging (airtight, opaque) also decide whether an active actually works.

3

Your Skin Type

Oily, dry and sensitive skin can all use the same ingredient, but they may need a different texture, strength or frequency.

4

Your Actual Concern

A "dark spot" could be an acne mark, sun damage or melasma, and each needs a different plan. Words like "brightening", "ubtan" or "glow" on the label are marketing, not ingredients.

The golden rule: introduce one new active at a time. Add the next one only after 2–4 weeks with no irritation. That way, if your skin reacts, you'll know exactly what caused it.

WHAT PAIRS WELL — AND WHAT DOESN'T
Niacinamide + almost anything Works alongside vitamin C, retinoids, azelaic acid and sunscreen Good together
Hyaluronic acid / Ceramides + any active Hydrate and cushion the skin, which makes actives easier to tolerate Good together
Vitamin C + Sunscreen Antioxidant plus UV filter is the strongest morning combination Good together
Vitamin C + Retinoid Both work, but use vitamin C in the morning and the retinoid at night AM / PM split
Retinoid + Salicylic / Glycolic acid Using both on the same night often leads to dryness, peeling and a damaged barrier Separate nights
Scrubs + acids + peels together Over-exfoliation is one of the most common reasons I see damaged barriers in clinic Avoid
DO'S & DON'TS
✔ Do
✔Patch test new products on your jawline for 2–3 days
✔Introduce one active at a time
✔Give each ingredient 8–12 weeks
✔Wear sunscreen every single morning
✔Moisturise, even if your skin is oily
✕ Don't
✕Layer 4–5 actives in one routine
✕Buy a product for the ingredient name alone
✕Copy someone else's routine, because their skin isn't yours
✕Use prescription-strength actives without guidance
✕Keep using anything that burns
WHEN TO SEE A DERMATOLOGIST

Ingredients have limits. See Dr. Pallavi if:

⚠Your acne is painful, deep or cystic, or it's leaving scars
⚠Dark patches on your cheeks or forehead keep coming back (possible melasma)
⚠You have constant redness, flushing or burning (possible rosacea)
⚠You see no improvement after 12 weeks of using the right ingredient consistently
⚠A spot is changing in size, shape or colour, bleeding, or not healing
⚠You're pregnant, breastfeeding or planning a pregnancy and want to start an active

The right ingredient helps, but the right diagnosis comes first. Some concerns need prescription treatment or in-clinic procedures to really improve.
